02 · Case Study · Governed Generative AI

From "AI is blocked" to a governed yes, without weakening a single control.

The full arc of the governed multi-agent retrieval case referenced on the main page: asset knowledge under probity conditions, in a data room where a wrong answer can bind a 15-year contract.

State government · Major rail franchise tender · Secure AI workflow in a probity-sensitive data room

Fifteen years of asset condition, maintenance obligations and performance data, where a wrong answer can bind a 15-year contract. The AI had to cite its source or stay silent.

Hands-on build & demo Approved & adopted Probity-sensitive Audit-ready
The problem

Refranchising a rail network means every asset condition claim, maintenance obligation and performance number in the data room can bind the state. Specialists fielded 100+ clarification questions per bidder, per month, each needing an evidence-backed, legally defensible answer drawn only from approved documents. Different reviewers could read the same evidence differently, which was a probity risk, not just an efficiency issue.

The environment

A controlled, audited data room, the single channel between franchise and bidders, version-controlled, every view logged. A zero-risk appetite environment with controls comparable to those expected in banking, infrastructure and public sector assurance. AI had been blocked outright as insecure; the work was changing that.

What I did

Recognised the pattern, educated stakeholders, and built a working prototype to demonstrate rather than pitch: a document-grounded, multi-agent workflow that routes each question to the right specialist team, retrieves only from approved sources, drafts with citations or abstains, then routes to a named human reviewer. I designed the retrieval flow, routing logic, abstain rules and review gates myself, then put the controls on and earned the approvals.

The decision under pressure

When AI was blocked as unsuitable for a secure environment, I did not bypass the concern. I reframed the problem around secure deployment, source control, access boundaries and mandatory human review. That moved the organisation from a blanket "no" to a governed "yes" without weakening a single risk control.

AI-based asset register verification

Asset registers drift from reality: assets get replaced, moved or decommissioned in the field and the record never catches up, and every predictive model built on a wrong register inherits the error. This prototype cross-checks the official rail station register for an entire state, 903 entries of open Vicmap data, against field mapping contributed independently by thousands of OpenStreetMap surveyors. It automatically matched 363 official records against independent open mapping and reduced 903 records to 91 candidates for engineering review.

62 operational records without a corresponding OpenStreetMap observation. 23 mapped stations without a corresponding register record. Four position discrepancies of more than 150 metres and two name conflicts. And a consistency check for free: 472 unmatched entries that the register itself marks as non-operational, where the method and the register agree.

Operational timetable system reconciled against the state asset register · open PTV GTFS and Vicmap data
Prototype · two official systems, one railway

AI-assisted systems integration

Every integration programme inherits this problem: multiple official systems describing the same assets differently. Here, the state's operational timetable system (3,484 records: platforms, entrances, replacement bus stops, wayfinding nodes) meets its 903-entry asset register. The pipeline classifies each record, collapses them to 319 real stations, and auto-reconciles 99% against the register. Two name variants resolved by confidence scoring, and exactly one conflict left for an engineer: one system says GEELONG SOUTH, the other South Geelong.

Why it matters for new networks

Months of manual reconciliation, reduced to a review queue

New lines, new operators and new alliances multiply the systems that must agree before anything runs: timetables, asset registers, maintenance systems, signalling data. Reconciling them by hand is where integration programmes lose months and inherit errors. Delivered at programme scale, this same discipline cut manual reconciliation by 60% and reporting cycles by 80% across a multi-operator platform, and the position histogram shows what it catches: the two systems disagree by more than 150 metres on four stations.

Compressor effort indicator against four documented air leak failures (red bands) · open MetroPT-3 dataset · operational metro train, Feb–Aug 2020
Prototype · open metro-train telemetry

Predicting failures days before they happen

Trains run on compressed air: brakes, doors and suspension all depend on it, and an onboard compressor keeps the system pressurised. When a leak develops, the compressor works harder and restarts more often to hold pressure. This model watches that effort across 1.5M real sensor readings from an in-service metro train and flags when it drifts above normal. It gave 5 to 14 days of warning before three of the four breakdowns in the operator's maintenance records. The chart above is the output.

The management principle, demonstrated

Checking whether maintenance actually worked

After the operator's documented repair on 30 May, the effort indicator never returned to normal, and the asset failed again six days later. The model caught an ineffective repair before the railway did. That is the frontier: AI that verifies whether maintenance restored the asset, not AI that generates more alerts.

05 · Governance in Depth

Asset assurance and AI assurance, held to the same standard.

This is the work I lead: asset management strategy, AI adoption in maintenance and performance, responsible AI controls, regulated implementation, and moving pilots into operational use. Fifteen years of asset governance taught me what assurance actually requires. The frameworks below are how I hold AI decisions to the bar asset decisions have always had to meet. I operate them, I do not just cite them.

ISO 55001Asset Management · Lead Auditor
The anchor. Fifteen years in asset governance and ISO 55001-aligned environments where governance failures had physical consequences. I link AI outputs to real asset decisions, lifecycle risk, whole-life cost and assurance obligations, with line of sight from a sensor reading to a board level renewal decision. This is the capability that separates asset AI that survives audit from asset AI that survives a demo.
ISO/IEC 42001AI Management System
Stand up the management system, assign roles and accountabilities, run lifecycle controls and governance cadence, and maintain the evidence packs that let AI use survive audit and scale across teams.
NIST AI RMFAI Risk Management Framework
Risk tier AI use cases, define harm scenarios, map controls to each tier, and monitor residual risk through the lifecycle, so governance scales with exposure instead of treating everything the same.
ISO/IEC 23894AI Risk Management
Convert AI risks into registers with named owners, mitigations, review triggers and clear accountability. That keeps risk active and managed, instead of just written down.
EU AI Act+ AU Responsible AI Principles
Use as a reference point for global enterprise AI risk classification, documentation, transparency and human oversight expectations, and translate Australian responsible AI principles into operational controls.
ISO/IEC 27001Information Security
Enforce access control, design the secure environment, classify information and keep every action auditable. This is the security spine that lets sensitive data anywhere near an AI system.
